GLSL int vs float — the results, explained

What the benchmark measured, what the numbers mean, and why a little retro-looking game cares about integer arithmetic inside your GPU.

The question

Every pixel a WebGL game draws is the output of a tiny program running on your graphics card — a shader. When that program has to multiply two colour values together, it can do the arithmetic in integers or in floating point. Integer arithmetic is exact but limited in range; floating point is smooth but can lose precision. Which is faster? It depends entirely on your GPU and its driver — and it's not always what you'd guess.

The game mimecroft (a maze game written in bash, rendered with WebGL) does its pixel colours in integers — a deliberately careful choice made years ago. This page summarises a benchmark that puts that choice to the test on a real browser GPU.

The results (one GPU: ANGLE → Direct3D 11, Intel UHD)

Each row renders the same work twice — once each way — and times both. The numbers below are from an Intel UHD laptop GPU driven through Chrome's ANGLE translator (the layer that converts WebGL into Direct3D).

The game was right all along. Its integer colour pipeline is ~1.6× faster than the equivalent floating-point version on this GPU — and its "mediump" precision is the faster of the two allowed levels. Even its choice of divisor (/128, a power of two, which the hardware can turn into a simple shift) is measurably faster than the "more correct" /127.

The last row is the most interesting: taking the game's actual compiled shader and rewriting every remaining floating-point step as an integer makes no measurable difference — because the expensive parts were already integer. The one part that is still floating-point (the colour tint) is so cheap that it doesn't matter.

Why this matters

  • Shaders run billions of times a second. A 1.6× difference in one multiply is a real frame-rate difference in a game that draws every pixel every frame.
  • The answer is not universal. The same shaders can rank differently on an AMD card, a phone's Mali GPU, or Apple's Metal translator. The numbers above are true for this one GPU — your GPU may differ.
  • Precision and speed are the same decision here. The game chose integers for exactness; the benchmark shows that exactness also happened to be the fast option.
